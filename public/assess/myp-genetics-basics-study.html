What is Genetics?

Genetics is the study of heredity and variation in living organisms. It explains how traits are passed from parents to offspring.

1. Genes: The Units of Inheritance

Genes are segments of DNA that control specific traits like eye color, height, or blood type.

Alleles: Different Versions of Genes

  • Allele: A variant form of a gene (e.g., B for brown eyes, b for blue eyes)
  • Homozygous: Two identical alleles (BB or bb)
  • Heterozygous: Two different alleles (Bb)

2. Chromosomes: Packages of DNA

Humans have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs). One set comes from each parent.

4. Sex-Linked Traits

Some traits are carried on the X chromosome. Males (XY) are more likely to express X-linked recessive traits.

Example: Color Blindness

  • More common in males (only one X chromosome)
  • Females need two copies of the recessive allele to be colorblind
  • Carrier females have one normal and one colorblind allele

  • Replicate: Makes copies of itself during cell division
  • Mutate: Changes in DNA can lead to evolution

6. Genetic Technology

Modern technology allows us to read, copy, and modify DNA!

🔬 PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction)

Laboratory technique to make millions of copies of a specific DNA segment.

🧪 Gel Electrophoresis

Separates DNA fragments by size. Used in forensics and paternity tests.

🧬 Genetic Modification

Adding genes to organisms to give them new traits (e.g., Bt corn resists insects).

7. Epigenetics: Nature vs. Nurture

Genes can be turned on or off by environmental factors without changing the DNA sequence!

  • Diet, stress, exercise can affect how genes are expressed
  • Methylation: Adding chemical tags that silence genes
  • Explains why identical twins can become different over time
Fun Fact: Your lifestyle choices can affect not only your gene expression but potentially your children's too!
