PCR, gel electrophoresis, DNA profiling, and GMO ethics

Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)

Definition: PCR is a technique used to amplify (make millions of copies of) a specific DNA segment in vitro (in a lab).

🔬 PCR Steps (Thermal Cycling)

  1. Denaturation (95°C): DNA double helix separates into single strands
  2. Annealing (55-65°C): Primers bind to complementary sequences on DNA
  3. Elongation (72°C): Taq polymerase adds nucleotides to extend new DNA strands

Result: 1 DNA segment → 2ⁿ copies after n cycles (e.g., 30 cycles = ~1 billion copies)

Key Components of PCR

  • DNA Template: The DNA containing the target sequence
  • Primers: Short DNA sequences that bind to start replication
  • Taq Polymerase: Heat-stable DNA polymerase from Thermus aquaticus
  • Nucleotides (dNTPs): Building blocks (A, T, G, C)
  • Buffer: Provides optimal conditions for the reaction

Gel Electrophoresis

Definition: A technique used to separate DNA fragments by size. DNA is negatively charged, so it moves toward the positive electrode.

🔬 Gel Electrophoresis Steps

  1. Prepare Agarose Gel: Melt agarose in buffer, pour into tray with comb
  2. Load DNA Samples: Mix DNA with loading dye, load into wells
  3. Run Current: Apply electric field (DNA moves toward positive anode)
  4. Stain and Visualize: Use ethidium bromide or SYBR Safe, view under UV light

Result: Smaller fragments move faster and travel farther; larger fragments stay closer to wells

Interpreting Gel Results

Feature Observation
Band Position Lower = smaller fragment; Higher = larger fragment
Band Intensity Brighter = more DNA; Fainter = less DNA
Ladder/Marker Known DNA sizes for comparison

DNA Profiling (Fingerprinting)

Purpose: Identify individuals based on unique patterns in their DNA (non-coding regions with repeats).

Steps in DNA Profiling

  1. Extract DNA from sample (blood, hair, skin)
  2. Amplify specific regions using PCR (STRs - Short Tandem Repeats)
  3. Separate fragments using gel electrophoresis or capillary electrophoresis
  4. Compare band patterns between samples
  5. Calculate probability of match by chance

Applications of DNA Profiling

  • Forensics: Crime scene investigation, identifying suspects
  • Paternity Testing: Determining biological parents
  • Medical Diagnosis: Identifying genetic disorders
  • Conservation: Tracking endangered species populations

Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs)

Definition: Organisms whose genetic material has been altered using genetic engineering techniques (recombinant DNA technology).

How GMOs Are Created

  • Identify desired gene (e.g., pesticide resistance)
  • Isolate and clone the gene
  • Insert gene into vector (plasmid)
  • Transfer to host organism (bacteria, plants, animals)
  • Select and test transformed organisms

Examples of GMOs

Organism Modification Purpose
Bt Corn Insert bacterial gene (Bt toxin) Resistance to insect pests
Golden Rice Added beta-carotene synthesis genes Combat vitamin A deficiency
Roundup Ready Soybeans Herbicide-resistant gene Tolerance to glyphosate herbicide

Ethical Implications of GMOs

⚠️ Concerns About GMOs

  • Potential health risks (allergies, unknown effects)
  • Environmental impact (gene flow to wild relatives)
  • Loss of biodiversity
  • Corporate control of seeds (patents)
  • Ethical concerns about "playing God"

✅ Benefits of GMOs

  • Increased crop yields and food security
  • Reduced pesticide use (Bt crops)
  • Enhanced nutritional content (Golden Rice)
  • Drought and salt tolerance
  • Medical applications (insulin production in bacteria)
