Epigenetics and environmental influences on gene expression

What is Gene Expression?

Definition: The process by which genetic information (DNA) is used to synthesize a functional gene product (usually a protein). Not all genes are expressed in every cell.

Key Concept

All cells have the same DNA, but different cell types (muscle, nerve, skin) express different sets of genes. Gene expression is regulated so that only needed proteins are produced.

Epigenetics

Definition: Heritable changes in gene expression that do not involve changes to the DNA sequence. Influenced by environment and lifestyle.

Epigenetic Modifications

DNA Sequence: ATGCTA → [Methylation] → Gene SILENCED

DNA Sequence: ATGCTA → [Acetylation] → Gene ACTIVATED

Common Epigenetic Mechanisms

1. DNA Methylation

  • Addition of methyl group (CH₃) to cytosine bases
  • Usually silences gene expression (turns gene OFF)
  • Occurs at CpG islands (promoter regions)
  • Can be inherited through cell divisions

2. Histone Modification

  • Histones are proteins that DNA wraps around
  • Acetylation: Loosens chromatin → gene turned ON
  • Methylation: Tightens chromatin → gene turned OFF
  • Changes accessibility of DNA to transcription machinery

Environmental Influences on Gene Expression

Key Idea: Environment and lifestyle can change gene expression through epigenetic modifications, without changing DNA sequence.

Examples of Environmental Factors

Factor Effect on Gene Expression Example
Diet/Nutrition Can methylate genes Folate deficiency → altered methylation patterns
Stress Alters cortisol-related gene expression Early life stress → lifelong changes in stress response
Smoking Changes methylation patterns Increased lung cancer risk via gene silencing
Exercise Activates beneficial genes Improved metabolism, brain health

Epigenetics and Disease

Cancer and Epigenetics

  • Cancer cells often have abnormal methylation patterns
  • Tumor suppressor genes can be silenced by methylation
  • Oncogenes can be activated by hypomethylation
  • Epigenetic drugs (e.g., demethylating agents) are used in cancer therapy

Other Diseases Linked to Epigenetics

  • Diabetes (imprinted genes affected)
  • Alzheimer's (altered histone modifications)
  • Autoimmune diseases (methylation changes)
  • Mental health disorders (stress-induced epigenetics)

Imprinting

Genomic Imprinting: Genes are expressed in a parent-of-origin-specific manner. Only the maternal OR paternal allele is expressed, not both.

Examples of Imprinted Genes

Gene Expressed Allele Disorder if Dysregulated
IGF2 (growth factor) Paternal allele only Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome
H19 (non-coding RNA) Maternal allele only Wilms tumor (kidney cancer)
SNRPN Maternal allele only Angelman syndrome
