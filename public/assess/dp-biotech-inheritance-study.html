Punnett squares, monohybrid crosses, and sex-linked traits

Monohybrid Crosses

Definition: A genetic cross examining the inheritance of a single trait (e.g., flower color, seed shape). Uses Punnett squares to predict offspring genotypes and phenotypes.

Example: Tall (T) vs. Dwarf (t) Pea Plants

Crossing two heterozygous parents (Tt × Tt):

T t
T TT (Tall) Tt (Tall)
t Tt (Tall) tt (Dwarf)

Genotypic Ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt

Phenotypic Ratio: 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf

Dominant vs. Recessive Alleles

Type Symbol Expression Example
Dominant Capital letter (T) Expressed in heterozygous (Tt) or homozygous (TT) Tall in pea plants
Recessive Lowercase letter (t) Only expressed in homozygous (tt) Dwarf in pea plants

Key Terminology

  • Homozygous: Two identical alleles (TT or tt)
  • Heterozygous: Two different alleles (Tt)
  • Genotype: Genetic makeup (TT, Tt, tt)
  • Phenotype: Physical appearance (Tall, Dwarf)

Sex-Linked Traits

Sex-linked genes: Genes located on sex chromosomes (X or Y). Most are X-linked because the X chromosome carries more genes.

🩸 Example: Hemophilia

  • X-linked recessive disorder
  • Gene for blood clotting factor is on X chromosome
  • Males (XY) express the trait if their single X carries the recessive allele
  • Females (XX) need two copies to express the trait
  • More common in males

cross: Hemophilia Carrier Mother × Normal Father

Mother: XᴺXʰ (carrier), Father: XᴺY (normal)

Xᴺ Xʰ
Xᴺ XᴺXᴺ (Normal female) XᴺXʰ (Carrier female)
Y XᴺY (Normal male) XʰY (Hemophiliac male)

Offspring: 25% normal female, 25% carrier female, 25% normal male, 25% hemophiliac male

Other Sex-Linked Examples

Disorder Type Inheritance Pattern
Red-Green Color Blindness X-linked recessive More common in males
Hemophilia A & B X-linked recessive More common in males
Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy X-linked recessive More common in males
Y-linked traits Y-linked Passed only from father to son

Steps to Solve Punnett Square Problems

  1. Determine the genotypes of the parents
  2. Identify the gametes each parent can produce
  3. Set up the Punnett square (rows = one parent's gametes, columns = other parent's gametes)
  4. Fill in all possible offspring genotypes
  5. Calculate genotypic and phenotypic ratios
  6. Answer the specific question asked
