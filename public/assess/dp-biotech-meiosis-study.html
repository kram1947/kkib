🔬 Meiosis (Topic 3.3)

Cell division producing haploid gametes with genetic variation

What is Meiosis?

Definition: Meiosis is a type of cell division that reduces the chromosome number by half, producing four haploid daughter cells (gametes). It involves two rounds of division: Meiosis I and Meiosis II.

Meiosis Overview

→
→

1 Diploid Cell (2n) → 4 Haploid Cells (n)

Meiosis I - Reduction Division

Chromosome number is halved. Homologous chromosomes separate.

Prophase I

  • Chromosomes condense
  • Homologous chromosomes pair (synapsis)
  • Crossing over occurs at chiasmata
  • Nuclear membrane breaks down

Metaphase I

  • Homologous pairs align at equator
  • Independent assortment (random orientation)
  • 2n chromosomes = 2² possible arrangements

Anaphase I

  • Homologous chromosomes separate
  • Sister chromatids remain attached
  • Chromosome number halves (2n → n)

Telophase I

  • Chromosomes arrive at poles
  • Cytokinesis occurs
  • Two haploid cells formed

Meiosis II - Separation of Sister Chromatids

Similar to mitosis. Sister chromatids separate.

Prophase II

  • Chromosomes condense again
  • No DNA replication
  • Nuclear membrane breaks down

Metaphase II

  • Chromosomes align at equator
  • Sister chromatids attached at centromere

Anaphase II

  • Sister chromatids separate
  • Move to opposite poles

Telophase II

  • Nuclear membranes reform
  • Cytokinesis occurs
  • Four haploid gametes produced

Sources of Genetic Variation

Meiosis creates genetic diversity through three mechanisms:

1. Crossing Over (Prophase I)

  • Exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes
  • Occurs at chiasmata
  • Creates new combinations of alleles
  • Increases genetic diversity significantly

2. Independent Assortment (Metaphase I)

  • Random orientation of homologous chromosome pairs
  • 2n chromosomes → 2ⁿ possible combinations
  • Humans: 2²³ = ~8.4 million combinations

3. Random Fertilization

  • Any sperm can fertilize any egg
  • Combines genetic material from two parents
  • Further increases variation

Meiosis vs. Mitosis

Feature Meiosis Mitosis
Purpose Produce gametes (sex cells) Growth, repair, asexual reproduction
Division Rounds Two (Meiosis I & II) One
Daughter Cells 4 haploid cells (n) 2 diploid cells (2n)
Genetic Variation High (crossing over, independent assortment) None (clones of parent cell)
Occurs In Reproductive organs (gonads) All body tissues
